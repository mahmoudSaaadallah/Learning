
### The Magic of Verbs: Who is Doing the Action?

[[Who is Doing the Action Io, Tu, Lui, Lei, Noi, Loro]].

In Italian, just like in English, we have words that tell us "who" is doing an action. These words are called **personal pronouns**. In English, they are "I, you, he, she, we, you (plural), they."

The "magic" in Italian is that the verb (the action, like "to walk" or "to swim") changes its **ending** depending on who is doing the action. This is called **conjugation**. It's like the verb adapts itself to the pronoun!


### Personal Pronouns and How to Use Them with Verbs

focusing first on "regular" verbs that end in `-are`, then we'll look at the others.

#### 1. Io (I)

-   **Meaning:** "Io" means "I".
-   **Rule for -ARE verbs:** When "Io" does the action, verbs that end in `-are` (like `camminare`, `nuotare`, `giocare`, `saltare`, `sudare`) drop the `-are` ending and take the ending **`-o`**.

| Infinitive Verb | Io + Conjugated Verb | Example Sentence | Translation |
| :-------------- | :------------------- | :--------------- | :---------- |
| `Camminare`     | `Io cammino`         | `Io cammino.`    | I walk.     |
| `Nuotare`       | `Io nuoto`           | `Io nuoto.`      | I swim.     |
| `Giocare`       | `Io gioco`           | `Io gioco.`      | I play.     |
| `Saltare`       | `Io salto`           | `Io salto.`      | I jump.     |
| `Sudare`        | `Io sudo`            | `Io sudo.`       | I sweat.    |

#### 2. Tu (You - informal, singular)

-   **Meaning:** "Tu" means "You", but we only use it with people we know well (friends, family, children). It's informal and singular (just one person).
-   **Rule for -ARE verbs:** When "Tu" does the action, verbs that end in `-are` drop the `-are` ending and take the ending **`-i`**.

| Infinitive Verb | Tu + Conjugated Verb | Example Sentence | Translation |
| :---------------- | :------------------- | :--------------- | :---------- |
| `Camminare`       | `Tu cammini`         | `Tu cammini.`    | You walk.   |
| `Nuotare`         | `Tu nuoti`           | `Tu nuoti.`      | You swim.   |
| `Giocare`         | `Tu giochi`          | `Tu giochi.`     | You play.   |
| `Saltare`         | `Tu salti`           | `Tu salti.`      | You jump.   |
| `Sudare`          | `Tu sudi`            | `Tu sudi.`       | You sweat.  |

#### 3. Lui (He) / Lei (She) / Lei (You - formal, singular)

-   **Meaning:**
    -   "Lui" means "He".
    -   "Lei" means "She".
    -   "Lei" (with a capital L) also means "You", but we use it with people we don't know well or want to show respect to (like a professor, a boss, an elder). It's formal and singular.
-   **Rule for -ARE verbs:** When "Lui", "Lei" (she), or "Lei" (formal you) do the action, verbs that end in `-are` drop the `-are` ending and take the ending **`-a`**.

| Infinitive Verb | Lui/Lei + Conjugated Verb | Example Sentence | Translation |
| :---------------- | :------------------------ | :--------------- | :---------- |
| `Camminare`       | `Lui cammina`             | `Lui cammina.`   | He walks.   |
| `Nuotare`         | `Lei nuota`               | `Lei nuota.`     | She swims.  |
| `Giocare`         | `Lui gioca`               | `Lui gioca.`     | He plays.   |
| `Saltare`         | `Lei salta`               | `Lei salta.`     | She jumps.  |
| `Sudare`          | `Lui suda`                | `Lui suda.`      | He sweats.  |
| `Camminare`       | `Lei cammina` (formal)    | `Lei cammina.`   | You walk (formal). |

#### 4. Noi (We)

-   **Meaning:** "Noi" means "We".
-   **Rule for -ARE verbs:** When "Noi" do the action, verbs that end in `-are` drop the `-are` ending and take the ending **`-iamo`**.

| Infinitive Verb | Noi + Conjugated Verb | Example Sentence | Translation |
| :---------------- | :-------------------- | :--------------- | :---------- |
| `Camminare`       | `Noi camminiamo`      | `Noi camminiamo.`| We walk.    |
| `Nuotare`         | `Noi nuotiamo`        | `Noi nuotiamo.`  | We swim.    |
| `Giocare`         | `Noi giochiamo`       | `Noi giochiamo.` | We play.    |
| `Saltare`         | `Noi saltiamo`        | `Noi saltiamo.`  | We jump.    |
| `Sudare`          | `Noi sudiamo`         | `Noi sudiamo.`   | We sweat.   |

#### 5. Voi (You - plural)

-   **Meaning:** "Voi" means "You" (plural), used for more than one person. It can be informal or formal, depending on the context.
-   **Rule for -ARE verbs:** When "Voi" do the action, verbs that end in `-are` drop the `-are` ending and take the ending **`-ate`**.

| Infinitive Verb | Voi + Conjugated Verb | Example Sentence | Translation |
| :---------------- | :-------------------- | :--------------- | :---------- |
| `Camminare`       | `Voi camminate`       | `Voi camminate.` | You all walk. |
| `Nuotare`         | `Voi nuotate`         | `Voi nuotate.`   | You all swim. |
| `Giocare`         | `Voi giocate`         | `Voi giocate.`   | You all play. |
| `Saltare`         | `Voi saltate`         | `Voi saltate.`   | You all jump. |
| `Sudare`          | `Voi sudate`          | `Voi sudate.`    | You all sweat. |

#### 6. Loro (They)

-   **Meaning:** "Loro" means "They", for both people and things, masculine or feminine.
-   **Rule for -ARE verbs:** When "Loro" do the action, verbs that end in `-are` drop the `-are` ending and take the ending **`-ano`**.

| Infinitive Verb | Loro + Conjugated Verb | Example Sentence | Translation |
| :---------------- | :--------------------- | :--------------- | :---------- |
| `Camminare`       | `Loro camminano`       | `Loro camminano.`| They walk.  |
| `Nuotare`         | `Loro nuotano`         | `Loro nuotano.`  | They swim.  |
| `Giocare`         | `Loro giocano`         | `Loro giocano.`  | They play.  |
| `Saltare`         | `Loro saltano`         | `Loro saltano.`  | They jump.  |
| `Sudare`          | `Loro sudano`          | `Loro sudano.`   | They sweat. |

### What about other verbs? (-ERE and -IRE)

There are also verbs that end in `-ere` (like `correre` - to run) and `-ire` (like `aprire` - to open). They have different endings, but the concept is the same: the verb changes to match the pronoun.

**Example with `Correre` (an -ERE verb):**

-   `Io corro` (I run)
-   `Tu corri` (You run)
-   `Lui/Lei corre` (He/She runs)
-   `Noi corriamo` (We run)
-   `Voi correte` (You all run)
-   `Loro corrono` (They run)

**Example with `Aprire` (an -IRE verb):**

-   `Io apro` (I open)
-   `Tu apri` (You open)
-   `Lui/Lei apre` (He/She opens)
-   `Noi apriamo` (We open)
-   `Voi aprite` (You all open)
-   `Loro aprono` (They open)

---

### Irregular Verbs: The "Exceptions"

Some verbs don't follow these rules. They are called **irregular verbs**. You'll need to memorize them, but don't worry, they are very common and you'll use them often, so they'll become easy with practice!

**Example with `Fare` (to do/make):**

-   `Io faccio` (I do)
-   `Tu fai` (You do)
-   `Lui/Lei fa` (He/She does)
-   `Noi facciamo` (We do)
-   `Voi fate` (You all do)
-   `Loro fanno` (They do)

**Example sentence:** `Io faccio esercizio.` (I do exercise.)

**Example with `Andare` (to go):**

-   `Io vado` (I go)
-   `Tu vai` (You go)
-   `Lui/Lei va` (He/She goes)
-   `Noi andiamo` (We go)
-   `Voi andate` (You all go)
-   `Loro vanno` (They go)

**Example sentence:** `Noi andiamo in bicicletta.` (We ride a bicycle.)

---

### Tips for an Absolute Beginner:

-   **Don't get discouraged!** It seems like a lot at first, but with practice, it will become natural.
-   **Focus on -ARE verbs first:** They are the easiest to start understanding the mechanism.
-   **Listen and Repeat:** Listen to Italian and try to repeat the sentences.
-   **Practice every day:** Even just 5 minutes a day makes a big difference.
-   **You don't always have to say the pronoun:** In Italian, we often understand who is doing the action from the verb ending, so it's not always necessary to say "Io", "Tu", etc. For example, `Cammino` already means "I walk". But to start, it's helpful to use them!
